# Goods rate evidence

**Covers**: the measured evidence behind the `goods rate` price series: pre-railway freight
rates, the statutory rate regime and its boundaries, the 1913–1922 transition, the measured
revenue per ton-mile series, and the modern ORR freight income data. The series itself, the
1900 anchor and the authoring method: [prices-goods-rate](prices-goods-rate.md).

Labels: **[M]** = figure transcribed from the cited source; **[D]** = derived here by arithmetic
on figures in the sources, stated explicitly; **[U]** = surfaced but not verified (OCR-garbled
or snippet only), not relied upon.

## Pre-railway rates, c. 1750–1835

### Statutory maximum tolls in canal and early railway Acts (d per ton per mile)

Parliament's authorised maxima, not charged rates: canal tolls were "only a charge for the use
of the waterway", carriage was a separate contract, and "how far the maximum tolls were in fact

## The statutory regime and the flat period (1830s–1913)

Source: Acworth 1905, chapters X–XII, unless stated.
`[LITERATURE:https://archive.org/details/elementsrailway00acwogoog]`

1. Every canal and railway authorising Act carried a statutory schedule of tonnage rates
   (classification + maximum tolls per ton per mile); unenumerated articles fell into the
   highest class (pp. 102–103, 110).
2. The early schedules "became obsolete at a very early date … forgotten or tacitly ignored"
   (p. 131); charges under the companies' own rules "were probably extra-legal, some were
   doubtless positively illegal" (p. 115).
3. From about 1845 "the statutory schedules became practically stereotyped"; until 1891 "railway
   history is concerned not with legislation, but rather with the almost independent action of
   the companies themselves" (p. 110). The Railway Clearing House (1850) built the working
   classification (pp. 111–113).
4. Railway and Canal Traffic Act 1888 s. 24: revised classification and maximum rates prepared
   per company; public inquiry by Lord Balfour of Burleigh and Sir Courtenay Boyle 1889–90
   (85 sitting days; 4,000-plus objections from 1,500-plus objectors
   `[LITERATURE:https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Economic_Journal/Volume_1/The_Regulation_of_Railway_Rates]`).
   35 Provisional Orders; nine confirmed 1891, twenty-six 1892; in force **1 January 1893**
   (Acworth pp. 134–135, 151).
5. Uniform statutory classification (A, B, C and 1–5); maxima = uniform terminal charges plus
   company conveyance maxima on the first 20 / next 30 / next 50 / remainder miles bands
   (p. 144). The Great Eastern schedule at p. 145 is the game's 1900 anchor
   `[USER-DIRECTION:2026-11-16]` (see [prices-goods-rate](prices-goods-rate.md)).
6. Charged rates sat well below the maxima: "rates, generally speaking, much lower than those at
   present authorized by Parliament" (Commissioners' report, quoted p. 139); exceptional rates
   "from 5 to 50 per cent, or even more, below" scale, and one general manager put only 15 per
   cent of business at scale rates (p. 126, c. 1885). The 1891–92 revision "left the rates at
   which the great bulk of the traffic was carried absolutely unaffected" (p. 148).
7. January 1893: special rates cancelled and class rates invoiced on the new schedules; outcry;
   Commons Committee 1893 found no justification; the Railway and Canal Traffic Act 1894 made
   any increase justifiable only on the company's proof (pp. 151–154). Acworth's conclusion:
   "The legislation of the years from 1891 to 1894 has done much to prevent any natural and
   gradual lowering of rates" (p. 158) — the regime pinned the level.
8. Independent statement of late-Victorian flatness: mineral working cost 2s 6.6d–2s 10.7d per
   train-mile in 1865 (Duke of Devonshire's Commission) and the same figures accepted in 1889:
   "from 1865 to 1889 there has been practically no reduction made in English railway rates"
   (Thompson p. 65). `[LITERATURE:https://archive.org/details/canalsystemofeng00thomuoft]`

**The "circa 1902" reform** `[RECOLLECTION:2026-11-16]`: no instrument of 1902–03 changing the
rate system was found in Acworth 1905 (contemporaneous), HANSARD 1902–05 or the secondary
sources searched. The documented reform sequence is 1888 → 1891/92 → 1893 → 1894. On user
direction the recollection is taken to refer to that sequence and the question is closed.
`[USER-DIRECTION:2026-11-16]`

Only official published per-ton-mile figures found for 1848–1922: gross receipts on controlled
railways, four weeks ended 29 February 1920 (before the 1920 increases): general merchandise
£1 1s 4d per ton and 2.759d per ton-mile; coal, coke, patent fuel 3s 5d and 0.910d; other
minerals 4s 9d and 1.114d. HANSARD 11 Aug 1920 **[M]**
`[LITERATURE:https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1920/aug/11/goods-traffic-receipts]`

Level comparison: coal Rotherham–London 6s 11d per ton (July 1914) → 10s 5d (July 1922),
+50.6% **[M]** `[LITERATURE:https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1922/jul/17/railway-rates]`.

Official ton-mile statistics begin with the Ministry of Transport era: four-weekly *Railway
Statistics* from 1920, annual returns from the 1919 return. The 1911 Act's prescribed form did
not require ton-miles (HANSARD 5 Jun 1919 `[LITERATURE:https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1919/jun/05/ton-mile-statistics]`).
The Board of Trade *Railway Returns* contain no ton-mile or per-ton-mile columns at all (checked
in the 1873 and 1907 volumes). Thompson (p. 67): "nor yet are there any available English
Railway Statistics as to the ton-mile rates (the railways themselves professing that they had
not the information)"; his own estimate "not less than 1½d. per ton per mile" c. 1900 is
**[M, estimated in source]**, not an official figure.

Per-ton sanity check (per ton, not per ton-mile): 1906 2s 4.6d and 1907 2s 4.5d
(goods receipts ÷ tons conveyed, derived from the *Railway Returns 1907* summary
`[LITERATURE:https://archive.org/details/railwayreturns00britgoog]`). The same summary marks
goods tonnage "Cannot be stated" for 1850 and 1870.

## Measured revenue per ton-mile, 1920–1977

### 1920–1931: blends from the monthly Ministry of Transport statistics

Derived (calendar-year sums of measured monthly values) as freight-train receipts ÷ net
ton-miles, in old pence per ton-mile: receipts series NBER m03084 (excludes collection and
delivery receipts 1920–1929), ton-miles NBER m03005a (includes free-hauled; new simplified
compilation from January 1922; 1920–July 1921 controlled railways only).
`[LITERATURE:https://data.nber.org/databases/macrohistory/rectdata/03/]`

| Year | d/ton-mile | Year | d/ton-mile |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1920 Jan–Aug | 1.413 | 1926 (strike) | 1.457 |
| 1920 Sep–Nov (increases in force) | 2.027 | 1927 | 1.398 |
| 1921 (coal stoppage) | 1.979 | 1928 | 1.405 |
| 1922 | 1.651 | 1929 | 1.360 |
| 1923 | 1.394 | 1930 | 1.341 |
| 1924 | 1.341 | 1931 | 1.331 |
| 1925 | 1.358 | 1938 (BR figure) | 1.34 |

The measured pre-increase 1920 blend divided by the documented wartime compound increase
(1.04 × 1.10 = 1.144) gives the pre-war level of 1.235d per ton-mile, which is the series
anchor: the derivation self-validates at the join. See
[prices-goods-rate](prices-goods-rate.md).

1939–1941: no ton-mile or per-ton-mile figures were ever published (Hultgren Table 32 gives
tons originated only). Documented charge steps instead: +5% Oct 1937; +10% over pre-war
1 May 1940; +16⅔% 1 Dec 1940; +25% 1 Jul 1946; +55% 1 Oct 1947 (of which +24% on freight-train
charges; HANSARD 5 Aug 1947); +16⅔% 15 May 1950; ≈ +175% over pre-war by 1956. The measured
average rose +30% from 1938 to 1942 against the +16⅔% charge step of Dec 1940 — mix and haul
effects sit on top of the charge changes.

cross-check only: freight revenue fell ≈40% in real terms 1967–1977.

The mix shift (MGR coal trains: 35/day 1967 → 148/day 1972) and the changing average haul sit
inside every blend figure. A fixed-mix index would be purer; per-category rates exist only in
fragments (the Feb 1920 table above).

## Modern data (1982–2026)

- Freight moved: ORR Table 1310 (annual, April 1982–March 2026, million net tonne-km; the
  ORR total excludes the separate infrastructure-traffic column; data to 1997-98 rounded to
  the nearest hundred million). `[LITERATURE:https://dataportal.orr.gov.uk/statistics/usage/freight-rail-usage-and-performance/]`
  Annual totals recorded in the session scratch; key years: 1982-83 16,600; 1984-85 11,800
  (miners' strike); 1994-95 13,000; 2013-14 22,700; 2015-16 17,756 (coal collapse); 2025-26
  16,055.
- Freight income, nominal, GB financial years (£m): 2015-16 890.2; 2016-17 789.8; 2017-18
  826; 2018-19 865.0 (per-year ORR report data tables, via the UK Web Archive); 2019-20 888.8;
  2020-21 787.9; 2021-22 870.0; 2022-23 961.1; 2023-24 1,001.9 (CPI de-deflation of Table 7243,
  reproducing the four published nominal years to within 0.3%); 2024-25 1,014.8 (ORR estimate).
  Definition: FTO statutory accounts, freight sales, excludes government funding.
  `[LITERATURE:https://dataportal.orr.gov.uk/statistics/finance/rail-industry-finance/]`
- **Table 7243 is CPI-deflated** (constant 2024-25 prices; three table vintages checked) and
  cannot go into a nominal series. The dataportal has never published nominal freight income.
- 1995-96 to 2013-14: **no free official freight income series exists publicly** (checked SRA
  *Rail Trends* 2003-04/2004-05, RDG industry data, ORR NRT yearbooks, ORR financial-information
  tables; McCartney & Stittle (2017) state freight finances "cannot be directly computed from
  published financial information"). Fallback: FTO statutory accounts at Companies House.
  `[LITERATURE:https://www.rug.ac.be/en/research/economics/research-papers]`
- Serpell (1982), Tables 1.5/1.6: revenue per net tonne-mile 4.3d (1975) → 4.8d (1982) **in
  1982 prices** — real, cross-check only.

## Open questions

- 1932–1937 goods receipts (ton-miles are measured): held in the annual *Railway Returns* for
  1937-38 (HathiTrust, public domain, page delivery blocked to scripts). Interpolation over
  these years is accepted; user direction 2026-11-16 is not to spend money closing small gaps.
  `[USER-DIRECTION:2026-11-16]`
- 1966, 1968–1971, 1973–1975, 1978–1994: sparse. RH072 (*Facts and Figures* 1980, GERS,
  £0.60) would cover 1978–79 if the gap ever matters; per user direction the gaps are left to
  engine interpolation.
- The Feb 1920 category blend: the HANSARD table gives per-category rates but not totals, so
  the category mix could not be computed; the anchor instead rests on the monthly totals and
  the documented percentage increases (see [prices-goods-rate](prices-goods-rate.md)).
- The 1922–23 Railway Rates Tribunal standard charges per class (TNA MT 66, print only) were
  never obtained; the 1920–23 movements are documented through the percentage changes and the
  measured blends instead.
