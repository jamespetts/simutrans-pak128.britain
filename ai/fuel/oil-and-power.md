# Fuel prices: oil, petrol, electricity and aviation

**Covers**: British gas oil, derv, petrol, industrial electricity, marine bunker and aviation fuel
prices, with the duty position and basis of each figure. Parent:
[fuel-evidence](../fuel-evidence.md). Coal and gas are in [coal](coal.md).

## Diesel, diesel oil and gas oil

Two distinct commodities appear in the British statistics and must not be merged. **Derv** (diesel
for road vehicles) is sold at the pump and bears full duty. **Gas oil** is the same distillation
fraction sold for industrial and heating use at a lower duty rate. Both are distinct from **heavy
fuel oil**, which is in section 6.

**Tax position.** In 2000 the pump price of premium unleaded was 79.93 pence per litre and the price
with VAT and duty removed was 19.61, so 60.32 pence per litre was tax (**computed**). In 2025 the
figures are 135.07 and 59.61, so 75.46 pence per litre was tax (**computed**).

**Break in series.** Four star was replaced by lead replacement petrol in October 1999 and that
series ended in September 2005; premium unleaded became ultra low sulphur petrol in April 2001. The
source states that pump prices for the old and new grades were broadly the same, but the grade
changes are not a like-for-like continuation and should be recorded as a basis change.

supplied by the supply industry, not average revenues. The 1993-2025 figures are the average price
paid by manufacturing industry, by size band, excluding the Climate Change Levy. The differential
between domestic and industrial is large wherever both are stated: in April 1976 the industrial
price was 75 per cent of the domestic price (**computed**: 1.5 ÷ 2.0).

A separate official table (S15, QEP 3.4.1) prices electricity to non-domestic consumers from 2004 on
a different sample and gives 3.871 pence per kWh for 2004 rising to 23.813 for 2025. Those values
differ from S8 for the years both cover, by up to 20 per cent, and must not be mixed into one series.

No price per kWh was located for the period before 1950, and none for a named municipal supply
undertaking or for the Electricity Commissioners' returns.

## Assessment

### Best long-run series by fuel

**Coal.** Two series carry the domain between them.

- *1750s to 1860s*: Clark and Jacks (S1), Table 1, column "'Pithead' average", decade averages,
  shillings per ton, northeast England, pithead. Twelve consecutive decades, a stated method and a
  stated standard error. This is the best long-run coal series found and the only one that covers
  the whole of the period the pakset needs for early steam. Its limits are that it is a northeast
  series, so it understates Welsh and London prices, and that it is built by regression from mixed
  quotations rather than read off a single return.
- *1925 to 1944*: the official "average proceeds per ton of coal disposable commercially" series,
  quoted in ministerial answers (S5a, S5c, S5d, S5e, S5g). Annual for Great Britain, with regional
  figures for Northumberland, Lancashire and Scotland at several dates. Producer's revenue at the
  pithead, not a delivered price.

The two do not meet. The nearest published overlap is Church's northeast pithead series for
1882-1913, which Clark and Jacks use as their link, but Church's table itself was not obtained.

**Gas.** No continuous series. Domestic average net selling value 1950-51 to 1977-78 (S7) and the
industrial price in pence per kWh 1993-2025 (S8) are both sound but do not join, and 1979-1992 is
empty in this file.

**Diesel and gas oil.** The strongest series in the file. DESNZ QEP table 4.1.2 gives annual retail
prices from 1977 to 2025 with an explicit and separately published tax-exclusive counterpart from
1990, so the duty element can be read off directly for 1990-2025. QEP table 4.1.3 extends the
January retail price back to 1954. S8 gives an industrial gas oil price in pounds per tonne for
1993-2025.

**Petrol.** The same two QEP tables, with the same tax split. Annual 1977-2025, January observations
1954-2025.

**Electricity.** Three blocks, none long: domestic average net selling value 1950-1977 (S7),
illustrative industrial tariffs 1974-1976 (S11, S12), and industrial average price 1993-2025 (S8).
The 1993-2025 block is the only one that states a delivered industrial price on a consistent basis
and is the right block for traction.

**Heavy fuel oil and bunkers.** Industrial heavy fuel oil in pounds per tonne 1993-2025 (S8). Bunker
coal free on board at two dates only (S6).

**Aviation.** IATA global jet kerosene in US dollars per barrel, 2019-2024 with the 2024 value a
forecast, and 2026 forecast (S13, S14). Not a British price and not a delivered price.

### Main gaps

1. **1861-1912 coal.** The largest hole and the one that matters most. There is no annual British
   coal price series in this file between the 1860s decade average and the 1913 pithead figure. The
   obvious fills are Church, *The History of the British Coal Industry*, vol. 3 (1986), pp. 52-59,
   which Clark and Jacks cite as their link series; Mitchell, *British Historical Statistics*
   (1988), chapter "Fuel and Energy", which begins at p. 236; and the South Wales pithead prices
   1878-1938 in the Welsh digest. All three are print or registration-gated.
2. **Railway contract coal.** No contract price paid by a railway company for locomotive coal was
   located for any year. Every coal figure here is a pithead price, a producer's proceeds or an
   industrial delivered price. The engine charges coal per kilogram consumed by the vehicle, so the
   delivered-to-depot price is the correct target and it is missing. The 1930s Hansard answers
   explicitly refuse to give a delivered average cost, on the ground that it varied with quality and
   carriage.
3. **1945-1992 in original units.** S8 begins in 1993. Earlier editions of DUKES carried the same
   table back to 1970 and the Ministry of Fuel and Power *Statistical Digest* carried it back to
   1938, but neither was obtained. The 1950-1977 coal, gas and electricity figures in this file are
   domestic retail or domestic revenue figures, not industrial prices.
4. **Town gas before 1950.** No price per therm or per thousand cubic feet for coal gas, and none
   for producer gas at any date. Gasworks' statutory maximum prices and the gas companies' charges
   are the likely source and were not reached.
5. **Electricity before 1950.** No price per kWh and no undertaking-level tariff. The Electricity
   Commissioners' returns and the area board tariffs are the stated route and neither was reached.
6. **Bunker oil and marine diesel.** Nothing at all. Bunker coal exists at two dates only.
7. **Aviation gasoline.** Nothing. The jet kerosene figures are a world market price in dollars,
   which cannot be compared with a sterling contract price without a conversion this file forbids.
8. **Regional coalfield prices in the twentieth century.** Only Northumberland, Lancashire and
   Scotland appear, and only as isolated dates. Welsh steam coal, which the pakset needs because
   Welsh coal was more expensive and had a different energy content, appears only as 1860 pithead
   and 1913 and 1930 free-on-board classes.

### Note on the existing `fuel[steam]` line

`New/config/fuel.tab` records the steam coal series as "based on English coal prices" with no
publisher, no unit and no basis, and `calibration-sources.md` lists that as an open question.

The values in that line correspond closely to the pithead series recorded here when read as
shillings per ton: the line gives 10 at 1900, 10 at 1913, 29 at 1919 and 46 at 1948, against
10s 1½d for 1913 and 29s 4d for 1919 in S4. That correspondence is an observation about the two
sets of numbers, not a source statement, and it does not establish the unit or the basis. It is
consistent with the line being a pithead price in shillings per ton, which is **not** the delivered
price the engine charges. This should be resolved before the steam series is next revised.
