# Vehicle capital costs: published sources

**Covers**: published purchase and build costs of British railway, road, sea and air
vehicles, with the specification each source states. Parent:
[vehicle-cost-evidence](../vehicle-cost-evidence.md).

Research note for the pakset vehicle cost calibration. Compiled 2026-11-16.

**Question.** What did a new British railway, road, sea or air vehicle cost to buy or build, by class
and era, and what did the buyer get for the money?

**Rules applied in this file.**

- One row per figure the source states.
- Price recorded in nominal pounds as printed, in the currency form the source uses (pounds,
  shillings and pence, guineas, dollars). Nothing converted, deflated, annualised, divided by
  weight or power, interpolated or estimated.
- The specification column carries only what the same source states on the same page. Where a
  source gives a price and no specification, the row says `none stated`; it is kept because the
  price is a usable data point, but it is the weakest kind of row.
- `Basis` distinguishes a build cost (the company's own works figure), a contract price, a
  tender price, a chassis price (vehicle without body), a list price (retail, sometimes before
  purchase tax) and a per-unit figure used inside an official report.
- `Primary` means the party that built or bought the vehicle recorded the figure: a works
  record, a company register, a price list, a parliamentary answer or a government report.
  `Secondary` means a historian, society or journalist states it, even where the text says the
  figure comes from a company record.
- Every figure below was read on the page cited. Where a page is an OCR transcription and the
  digits are doubtful, the row says so.

## Assessment

### What is well covered

**Road vehicles, 1923 to 1980.** This is the strongest domain, and it is strong for a specific
reason: The Commercial Motor printed chassis prices beside full specifications as a matter of
routine, and its archive is full-text searchable. Every road row above except the cars carries
engine size, power, wheelbase and payload or seating from the same page as the price. The
distinction between chassis price and complete vehicle price is visible in the data (Bedford SB
chassis £690 in 1950 against £3,620 complete with a 41-seat body in 1957), which matters for the
pakset because the engine's `cost` field prices a whole vehicle.

**Industrial and contractor locomotives, 1940.** The Industrial Railway Society archive pages
reproduce works records with dimensions, weight and tractive effort on the same page as the price.
The Bagnall "Hawarden" row is the best-specified single locomotive figure in this file.

**Railway carriages, 1842 to 1888.** Whishaw's 1842 descriptions and the LBSCR and LYR carriage
registers give cost, length, seating and tare together. These are the rows to use for the
pre-grouping carriage cost curve.

**Locomotive contract prices, 1850 to 1882.** The RCTS-derived SteamIndex pages give per-class
contract prices with cylinder and wheel dimensions and, often, the number built in the batch. They
are secondary, but they quote company stock books and the numbers are specific enough to check.

**BR capital costs, 1951 to 1966.** The Beeching unit costs, the Hansard cost per 1,000 draw-bar
h.p. and the 1960 Type 4 tender comparison together give a defensible ladder for the diesel
transition. The 1960 tender comparison is unusual and valuable: five builders' prices for one
specification in one year.

### What is thin

**Locomotive prices, 1883 to 1939.** Almost nothing with both a price and a weight. The main-line
companies built in their own works and charged the cost to capital account without publishing a
per-engine figure; the builders' contract prices survive mainly in class histories, and those
that are online state the price but rarely the weight. The gap is the most serious one for the
pakset, because it is the core of the steam era.

**Carriages and wagons, 1889 to 1967.** One LYR figure for 1888 and then nothing until Beeching's
£3,000 generic wagon in 1963. The inter-war and BR wagon prices, which matter for the freight
side of the pakset, are absent.

**Ships.** The Caledon contract prices are genuine primary figures but the transcription gives
only type and official number, not tonnage or dimensions, so they cannot be scaled. Only the
Fairplay series carries a defined size, and it is a price per ton of one reference ship, not a
price for a named vessel.

**Aircraft.** Seven rows, of which five are secondary statements of a "price" with no procurement
basis. This is the weakest domain in the file and should not be used to set aircraft costs
without more work.

**Anything before 1830, and anything after 1980.** Deliberately excluded: the brief puts
post-1988 into the index series, and no reliable pre-1830 vehicle price was found.

### Where more is likely to be found

1. **The Commercial Motor archive, systematically.** Only about a dozen articles were read here.
   The archive runs from 1905 and carries a chassis price in most road tests and in its annual
   buyers' guides. A sweep of one buyers' guide per decade would give a continuous road vehicle
   series with specifications, which is the single highest-return follow-up available.
2. **The Industrial Railway Society archive.** The Shelton article was one of a numbered series of
   archive pages, each reproducing works records. They contain tender prices, order prices and
   main dimensions together. The same society's "Sundry Notes and Queries" pages also record
   second-hand purchase prices with cylinder dimensions.
3. **Railway company carriage and wagon registers held by the learned societies.** The LBSCR
   Society publishes per-vehicle cost columns; the LYR Society publishes diagram books and cost
   notes; the Great Eastern Railway Society publishes wagon cost build-ups. These are the
   practical route to the 1889-1939 carriage and wagon gap, and they are primary records.
4. **Hansard, 1945-1970.** Parliamentary answers carry capital cost per unit of power, per
   locomotive and per ship, and the historic Hansard pages are readable one debate at a time.
   The 1951 answer used above shows the form; there will be equivalents for diesel classes,
   freight wagons and aircraft procurement. The site has no working search API, so this needs
   either a search engine restricted to hansard.parliament.uk or a session on the historic
   Hansard index pages.
5. **Board of Trade and British Transport Commission annual accounts.** Not reached in this
   session. They give rolling stock capital expenditure and vehicle numbers together, which is
   the "number of vehicles and the total" form the brief asks for first. They are in the
   parliamentary papers series, not on the open web.
6. **Builder order books: Beyer Peacock, North British, Vulcan Foundry, Metropolitan-Cammell.**
   The Manchester Museum of Science and Industry, the Glasgow University Archive Services
   (Scotts, Beardmore, Fairfield) and the Newcastle Marine Technology Special Collection
   (Swan Hunter contract lists, 1861-1993) all hold contract and cost books. The Scotts
   collection explicitly includes "Cost books 1825-1978" and "Enquiries, tenders, contracts and
   specifications 1853-1974". These are archive visits, not web pages, but they are where the
   missing 1883-1939 locomotive and ship data actually is.
7. **Ship dimensions to pair with the Caledon prices.** The same vessels appear in
   shippingandshipbuilding.uk with gross tonnage, length, breadth and engine detail. Pairing the
   two sources by yard number would turn the Caledon rows into fully specified ones without any
   estimate.
