# Inland boat maintenance

**Covers**: repair costs for inland and canal boats, chiefly individual repair jobs from canal
company records, plus modern narrowboat figures. Parent:
[vehicle-cost-evidence](../vehicle-cost-evidence.md). Sea-going ships are in
[ship-maintenance](ship-maintenance.md).

## 1. Grand Junction Canal Company tunnel tugs, 1877-1928

Source for F34-F46: extracts from the Grand Junction Canal Company minutes (The National Archives,
PRO RAIL 830/26-830/47) and from "PRO RAIL 830/80, Extracts from the Tunnel Tugs Expenses Book",
transcribed at http://steamershistorical.co.uk/steamers_GJCC_Minutes_03.htm. The craft are the
Company's tunnel tugs Hasty, Spider, Pilot, Badger and deSalis, and the steam launch Swift. These
are canal company craft, not narrowboats and not wide boats. `[P]` (transcription of the minutes;

## 4. Inland boats: what was searched and not found

- **No published annual repair and maintenance figure for a working narrowboat or wide boat was
  found.** Not per boat-year, not as a percentage of boat capital, not per ton-mile.
- The records that would yield one exist and are catalogued, but are not digitised with figures:
  Grand Union Canal Carrying Co. Ltd account books 1939 (Canal & River Trust, BW58/3/1/7) and
  general ledgers 1929-1952 (BW58/3/1); monthly and yearly expenditure records of the Grand Union
  Canal Carrying Co., Thomas Clayton (Paddington) and Erewash Canal Co. (BW58/3/1/11, 1941);
  Fellows, Morton & Clayton accounts 1908-1909 and "docking book 1940s" (BW118); the Polesworth
  Dock ledger of boats hired, repaired and built, 1921-1941 (BW153/56/2); Grand Junction Canal
  Company capital expenditure on "compartment boats" and the carrying trade, 1858-1885
  (BW91/4/2/6). Catalogue entries: https://collections.canalrivertrust.org.uk/bw58 and /bw118
  `[P]` (descriptions of holdings, no figures).
- The Grand Union Canal Company's own published statements do not separate boat maintenance. The
  Chairman's statements quoted in the company history refer to cutting maintenance costs by
  reducing the number of pairs in commission from 1937, and to toll receipts being "insufficient to
  pay the cost of even a moderate programme of repairs and renewals" (1945), without figures for
  boats as distinct from the navigation.
- A canal carrying cost of "¾d per ton per mile" including tolls is quoted for a motor boat in a
  compilation on the Birmingham Canal Navigations ("A-Z of BCN"), but the vessel, date and scope
  are not established in the accessible text, so it is not recorded as a figure here.

# Assessment

**Directly usable, ships.**

1. F1-F8 (OpCost 2004 handysize bulker) are the strongest single block. They give repair and
   maintenance separated from spares, from lubricants, from stores, from insurance and from crew,
   on a stated sample, for a stated year, per vessel-year and per vessel-day. This is the only
   source found that separates all six heads for one vessel.
2. F14-F21 (Drewry 1991-1998) give repair and maintenance per day by size for two ship types over
   eight years, and the share of daily operating cost. They are marked unverified because the
   carrying PDF could not be opened; if the eight rows are needed, the document should be obtained
   directly from the World Maritime University repository before use.
3. F9-F13 are total operating cost only. Usable as a denominator, not as repair.
4. F22 gives a docking cost per docking with a clear population (six car carriers, one LPG, one
   bulker) and a clear date. Usable with the caveat that it covers one manager's fleet.
5. F23 is the cleanest statement in the file of the difference between spend and provision: the
   cost is incurred at the docking and then amortised over the 2.5-year cycle. The balance at
   31 December 2023 is a deferred charge, not a reserve and not spend in that year.
6. F24 and F25 are the survey intervals, from the rule book rather than from a commentary.
7. F29, F30, F32 are usable single points for 1849, 1862 and 1913, each with a narrow, stated
   scope. None of them is a whole-vessel repair cost except F32, which is an overhaul figure for a
   very large liner and should not be extended to small craft.
8. F33 is usable only as evidence of what company accounts print, not as a repair figure.

**Not usable as a repair cost.** F27 and F28 are survey fees, not repairs; they belong to the
"survey fees" component only. F31 is a one-off outfitting cost on purchase. The OpCost indices in
A2 are index numbers.

**Can a per-voyage repair cost be constructed?** Not from published sources. No source found gives
repair cost per voyage, per ton of cargo carried, or per ton-mile, for any year, British or
otherwise. A per-voyage figure can be constructed by dividing a per-vessel-year figure (F1, F2,
F4, F14-F21) by the number of voyages or voyage days, but that is a construction, not a measurement,
and the divisor is not in these sources. The primary records that would support a measured
per-voyage figure exist at P&O (ships' cost and repair ledgers 1855-1941; summaries of ships'
voyage expenses 1881-1890) and at the Canal & River Trust for the canal carriers, and are not
published. For the inland trade the same applies: the Grand Junction Canal Company expenses book
(F34-F46) is per job, not per voyage, and gives no cargo tonnage against which to divide.

**Most of the historical range is thin.** The file has four sea-going points before 1920 (1849,
1862, early 1870s, 1913) and nothing between 1914 and 1991 except the survey rules. It has no
British liner company repair figure for any year. The
reason is structural, not a failure of search: British companies did not publish the line, and the
ledgers that contain it are archival.

**Inland boats: yes, covered, but only at the level of individual repair jobs.** F34-F47 are
British, dated, and state what each payment covered: painting, hull repair, boiler renewal, new
hull, complete new craft. That is enough to establish the shape of inland craft maintenance (hull
painting at a few pounds, running repairs at a few pounds to about £17, a new hull at £200-£420, a
complete new tunnel tug at £1,065 in 1923). It is not enough to state an annual maintenance rate
per boat, and no such rate was found in any source. **No figure for a narrowboat or wide boat
working in the carrying trade was found at all**; the closest inland figures are canal company
tunnel tugs and a canal company ice boat, which are different craft with different duty. The modern
narrowboat figures (F48-F51) are for pleasure craft in 2020 and should not be projected backwards.

**One caution on the inland block.** F34-F46 rest on a single volunteer transcription of National
Archives minutes; the sums are internally consistent and the minute references are given, but the
original volumes at Kew have not been checked.

**Open questions.**

1. Does any published British source give repair cost per gross ton per annum for a tramp steamer
   between 1900 and 1939? The Kirkaldy accounts do not, and the obvious company histories were not
   reachable in full text.
2. What did the Grand Union Canal Carrying Company actually spend per boat per year? The answer is
   in BW58/3/1/7 and BW58/3/1/11 and would need a visit to the Canal & River Trust archive.
3. Is there a narrowboat blacking interval recorded before 1945, as distinct from the modern
   two-to-three-year practice?
