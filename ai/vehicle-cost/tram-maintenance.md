# Tram and light rail maintenance costs, British

**Covers**: tram and light rail maintenance costs in Britain, historical and modern, with the
accounting head and denominator of each figure. Parent:
[vehicle-cost-evidence](../vehicle-cost-evidence.md).

Compiled 2026-11-17 for transport cost calibration. Evidence only: no conversion, no
deflation, no derived ratio and no estimate, except where a row is explicitly marked
**computed**. Every figure is recorded in the unit the source prints. Where a source gives no
denominator, that is stated rather than supplied.

Abbreviations: d. = pence (pre-decimal where the year is pre-1971); £k = thousand pounds;
£m = million pounds; car-mile = one car (tram) run one mile, trailers counted as cars in the
Ministry returns; P/S = primary or secondary.

Two warnings that apply to the whole file:

- **Tram and trolleybus are different vehicles.** Where a source reports both, the rows say
  which is which. Trolleybus figures are included only where the source itself compares them
  with trams on the same route.
- **"Maintenance" in the Ministry of Transport returns is a combined head.** It is not car
  maintenance alone. See the head note to section A1.

## A. Old trams, c.1870-1950

### A1. Ministry of Transport annual returns: maintenance per car-mile, 1913-14 to 1930-31

The annual statutory returns of tramway undertakings print a comparative table headed
"Receipts or Expenditure per Car Mile (in pence)". Its expenditure side has six heads:
General Repairs and Maintenance; Power Expenses; Traffic Expenses; Rates and Taxes;
Compensation and Accident Insurance; General Expenses. Gross working expenditure is the sum
of the six, and the printed gross has been checked against the six values in every row below;
where the six did not sum to the printed gross, the row has been left out (one row, local
authorities 1922-23).

**Head note, applying to every row in this table.**

- The head is printed as "General Repairs and Maintenance". The return's detailed accounts carry
  separate columns for permanent way, electrical equipment of line, and cars and equipment of
  cars; the comparative table prints only the combined figure. The figure therefore **includes
  way and works, line electrical equipment and car maintenance together**, and cannot be read as
  car maintenance alone.
- **Excluded** by construction: power (separate head), traffic expenses (separate head; the
  summary does not state which staff costs it contains), rates and taxes, compensation and
  accident insurance, general expenses.
- **Depreciation and renewals:** appropriations to reserve, renewal and depreciation funds, and
  "expenditure during the year on renewals charged to funds", are printed in a separate part of
  the return, not in the maintenance head. Whether repair labour and materials charged to
  capital are inside the head is not stated.
- **Tyres** (wheel tyres) are not named in the head; whether they are inside it is not stated.
- **Denominator:** car-miles run, defined by the return as "the total number of miles run
  during the time cars have been engaged in service work", with trailer miles counted as car
  miles.
- These are nominal pence of the year shown. No price index has been applied.

### B4. Sources found but not used

- **Edinburgh Trams statutory accounts 2024.** The published PDF (48 pages) has no text layer;
  text extraction returns 902 characters for the whole document. A search index of the file
  shows a narrative statement that traction cost rose from £2.614m in 2023 to £3.6m in 2024 and
  that mileage rose by 220,000 km to 1.895m km, and that CAF is responsible for tram
  maintenance, but the figures could not be read from the document itself and are therefore not
  recorded as evidence here. OCR of the file would recover them.
- **Halcrow, Malta Light Rail Study (2008).** The report's Table 6.1 is indexed as giving
  vehicle maintenance cost of 1.49 and 1.53 per 1,000 vehicle-km for American and European
  system averages, and section 5.3.4 as giving €0.45/km for electric and €0.56/km for diesel
  tram vehicle maintenance "including all staff and equipment costs, but not depot capital
  costs". The file returns HTTP 403 to direct download, so the figures were not verified and
  are not recorded.
- **Transport for Greater Manchester, Statement of Accounts 2025/26.** Prints Metrolink net
  expenditure of £110.033m for 2025/26. This is the whole contracted operation and maintenance of
  the network net of income, not a maintenance figure, so it is not entered as a maintenance
  record.

## Assessment

### Directly usable

- **The 26 per-car-mile rows in A1** are the strongest historical evidence in this file. They
  are one head, one definition and one denominator, from the statutory returns, for nine years
  between 1913-14 and 1930-31, split into local authorities, companies and total. The internal
  check (six heads summing to the printed gross working expenditure) passed on every row
  included. For a tram `runningcost` calibration they give a defensible level and, more usefully,
  a shape: a post-1918 peak in 1922-23, a fall to 1925-26, then a flat plateau at about 3d. per
  car-mile from 1927-28 to 1930-31.
- **The two annual totals in A2** pair a stated £ maintenance figure with a stated car-mileage in
  the same document and the same year. They are the cleanest single-year observations in the
  historical set.
- **The Supertram rows in B1** are the only modern figures found that separate rolling-stock
  maintenance from infrastructure maintenance in pounds, for a named operator, with a second
  year for comparison. They are budget figures, not outturn, and the plan says so.

### Usable with a caveat

- **Every A1 row is an upper bound on car maintenance, not car maintenance.** The head includes
  permanent way and line electrical equipment. The return's detailed accounts carry permanent
  way, electrical equipment of line, and cars and equipment of cars as separate columns, which is
  why the combined head cannot be treated as a vehicle cost. A pakset running cost set from 3d.
  per car-mile would therefore charge the vehicle for track as well.
- **The 1922-23 companies and total rows** come from a different annual return from the rest and
  are the year before the 1923 standardisation of tramway accounts; the 1925-26 return prints
  them in the same table, but the definitional continuity across 1922-23 is not demonstrated.
- **The West Hartlepool rows** are a single small undertaking, one year, and the tram figure is
  explicitly mostly permanent way. They are useful as a worked example of how the combined head
  behaved at undertaking level, not as a level for calibration.
- **The Ashton rows** are an officer's spoken approximation ("about 2d.", "about 1d."), reported
  in the trade press. Treat as order-of-magnitude only.
- **The Supertram percentages (11% maintenance, 12% traction)** are shares of a budget, not unit
  costs, and the maintenance share covers trams, track and infrastructure together.
- **The DfT mileage rows** are denominators only. Pairing them with Supertram's £1,647k rolling
  stock maintenance for 2025/26 and 1.2m vehicle miles gives about £1.37 per vehicle-mile
  (**computed**, £1,647,000 ÷ 1,200,000); the two figures come from different documents and the
  DfT figure is rounded to 0.1m miles, so the result carries both errors. It is recorded here as
  an illustration, not as a measured unit cost.
- **The DLR rows** give direction and rank, no level. They cannot calibrate anything on their
  own.

### Gaps this file does not close

- **No car-only historical maintenance figure.** The statutory returns combine the head, and the
  municipal accounts that were checked either combine it or report only totals. A car-only
  figure needs a corporation's own abstract of accounts, where "repairs and maintenance of cars"
  is a separate line, or an engineering paper that tabulates car maintenance separately.
- **No modern per-vehicle-km maintenance figure stated as such.** Supertram gives £ totals;
  DfT gives vehicle miles; nobody found states the ratio. The ratio has to be computed, with the
  caveats above.
- **Edinburgh, Metrolink, NET, West Midlands Metro, Croydon and Tyne and Wear have no
  maintenance figure in this file at all.** Their accounts either do not split maintenance or
  were not readable without OCR.

### Most productive sources if followed up

1. **The Ministry of Transport annual returns for the years between 1900 and 1922** (the same
   series as A1, of which the 1922-23, 1925-26, 1928-29, 1929-30 and 1930-31 volumes are on
   archive.org as `railways00590412`, `railways00609100`, `railways00609133`, `railways00609101`).
   They would extend the per-car-mile series back through the electric-tramway conversion and
   give the horse and steam years on the same definition. The 1922-23 volume in particular would
   fill the missing local-authorities row.
2. **The detailed accounts in the same returns**, which do print "permanent way", "electrical
   equipment of line" and "cars and equipment of cars" as separate columns. A careful reading of
   the printed page (not the OCR) would give a car-only maintenance total for 1930-31 and
   possibly for other years. The digitised copy at ekeving.se is a page image with an OCR layer
   that misaligns the columns; the printed page needs to be read.
3. **Municipal abstracts of accounts** for Manchester, Birmingham, Glasgow, Liverpool, Leeds,
   Sheffield, Bradford, Edinburgh and Belfast. These are the route to a car-only head. They are
   not on archive.org as a rule and would need the corporation's own annual abstract or a local
   studies collection.
4. **The Tramway and Railway World volumes 1913-1930** (archive.org identifiers `trawo-33` to
   `trawo-68`), which reported municipal accounts in detail and may carry the car-only head in
   their annual statistical issues. The text files for several volumes were not retrievable in
   this session; the volumes exist as items.
5. **Edinburgh Trams' statutory accounts**, after OCR. They name the three maintenance contracts
   (vehicle maintenance with CAF, ticketing with Flowbird, infrastructure with Siemens/Bilfinger)
   and would give a modern vehicle-maintenance charge for a 27-tram fleet.
6. **The CoMET/Nova benchmarking material behind the TfL report**, which holds absolute
   maintenance cost per car-km by asset class for DLR and for peer metros. The published TfL
   report presents it as charts only; the underlying figures are what would make a modern
   benchmark.
