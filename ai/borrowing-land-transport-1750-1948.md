# Non-railway transport borrowing, 1750-1948: land transport

**Covers**: the rates at which turnpike trusts, canal companies, omnibus companies and tramway
companies borrowed, 1730 to 1948. Parent: [cost-of-capital](cost-of-capital.md). Docks,
shipping, the short end and the general industrial benchmark are in
[borrowing-docks-shipping-1750-1948](borrowing-docks-shipping-1750-1948.md).

## Table 1 — Turnpike trusts

Turnpike borrowing was by bond secured on the tolls ("mortgaged debt") or unsecured ("floating
debt"), with no set maturity in the usual case. Both sources that state the legal position give 5
per cent as the maximum rate permitted by the usury laws.

| ID | Year | Borrower | Instrument and security | Rate % and type | Term | Source | P/S |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | 1730-1830 | 100 turnpike trusts (Albert's sample) | Mortgage bonds, secured on tolls | 4.66, average of the rates stated for the sample | No fixed maturity | BOGART p. 9, reporting Albert (1972) pp. 247-261 | Secondary |
| F2 | 1820 | All turnpike trusts, England and Wales | Mortgage and floating debt | 3.94, rate paid (interest paid as per cent of debt) | — | BOGART Table 1 Panel A, from BPP 1821 IV | Secondary |
| F3 | 1829 | As F2 | As F2 | 3.44, rate paid | — | BOGART Table 1 Panel A, from BPP 1840 XXVII | Secondary |
| F4 | 1838 | As F2 | As F2 | 4.04, rate paid | — | As F3 | Secondary |
| F5 | 1842 | As F2 | As F2 | 4.09, rate paid | — | BOGART Table 1 Panel A, from BPP 1844 XLII | Secondary |
| F6 | 1848 | As F2 | As F2 | 3.95, rate paid | — | BOGART Table 1 Panel A, from BPP 1851 XLVIII | Secondary |
| F7 | 1838 | All turnpike trusts, England and Wales | Contracted rate on the debt | 5.00, contracted rate, held by 75 per cent of trusts, this being the usury maximum; 4.00 or 4.50 held by 24 per cent | — | BOGART p. 3 and p. 10 | Secondary |
| F8 | 1817-1832 | 107 turnpike trusts borrowing from the Public Works Loan Board | Mortgage loan, PWLB taking first charge | 5.00, rate charged, this being the maximum allowed by the usury laws | — | BOGART p. 8 | Secondary |

**Notes on Table 1**

1. F2-F6 are interest actually paid divided by total debt, across the whole population of trusts.
   They are therefore below the contracted rates in F7, because some trusts paid nothing. The
   same table adds unpaid interest to the debt: on that basis the rate paid is 3.47 (1820), 3.07
   (1829), 3.47 (1834), 3.51 (1838), 3.45 (1842), 3.37 (1845) and 3.22 (1848).
2. Bogart states that 15 per cent of trusts paid no interest in 1820 and 12 per cent in 1838, and
   that 60 per cent paid more than 4.5 per cent in 1820 and 55 per cent in 1838. The dispersion
   is therefore wider than the averages in F2-F6.
3. F1 is the only figure found that covers turnpike bonds before 1820.

**Notes on Table 2**

1. F10-F13 are the only canal borrowing rates found before 1870. F9 is included as the statutory
   rate allowed on capital advanced, which is not a borrowing rate; it is recorded because it
   fixed the return a canal proprietor expected and therefore the terms on which a canal could
   raise money.
2. The Exchequer Bill rates in F11-F13 are government lending rates, set by statute, not market
   rates. Boyes states that these rates were "almost always higher than the market rates" and
   that the Commissioners "generally only attracted applications from canal and railway
   undertakings which had difficulty in raising finance in the open market". Three canal
   companies (Gloucester and Berkeley, Edinburgh and Glasgow Union, Birmingham and Liverpool
   Junction) and the Liverpool and Manchester Railway were given a reduction to 3.50 per cent on
   all or part of their loans.
3. F10 is corroborated by the same minutes: in 1802 the company paid "a sum equal to 2 per cent
   on the company's debt of £104,425 due on mortgage and on £12,700 due on bond amounting to
   £117,125" on account of interest then due, which shows that the 5 per cent authorised in 1796
   was not being met in full.
4. F14's stock was authorised by the Grand Junction Canal Act, 1879 (reported in TRING, quoting
   the company's records, not in a primary source consulted here); the 1908 Year-Book confirms
   the 4 per cent perpetual stock in issue.
5. The Manchester Ship Canal's £5,000,000 new mortgage debentures held by Manchester Corporation
   carried interest reduced by the Act of 1904 "from 4½ to 3½ per cent"; they are excluded from
   the table because the holding was statutory and non-transferable. The same entry records
   £448,000 of 4 per cent first mortgage debentures, which the optical-character text runs
   together with the £1,359,000 perpetual stock; the two are recorded separately here only as
   far as the text allows, and the description "first mortgage" is attached to the £448,000
   issue, not to the £1,359,000.

**Notes on Table 3**

1. F20 and F22 are the only omnibus-company debenture rates found before 1914. Both are 4 per
   cent, and both were secured on property. F20 was issued at a premium in every tranche, so the
   yield to the buyer was below the coupon; the source does not state the yield and none is
   computed here.
2. No rate was found for a stage coach proprietor. The evidence located describes the
   organisation, not the price of money: coaches were supplied by coachbuilders who carried the
   capital risk and leased them to operators at a rate per double mile, and the horses were
   provided by partner innkeepers out of their own capital. The contemporary sources examined
   state the leasing rates and the capital values but no interest rate. This is recorded as a gap
   in the Assessment.

## Table 4 — Tramway companies

Tramway companies borrowed by debenture stock, usually with a floating charge or a specific
charge on the tramway, the shares of subsidiary companies, or the depots. Borrowing powers were
commonly limited by statute or order to one-half of the paid-up share capital.

**Notes on Table 4**

1. The range of tramway debenture coupons in issue in 1908 is 3.50 to 5.00, with 4.00 and 4.50
   the most common. F25-F34 record one instrument for each of ten companies. The extracted text
   of the same section records debenture rates for at least twenty-five further companies,
   including 4.00 for Cork Electric Tramways, 4.50 for Coventry Electric Tramways, 4.00 for
   Devonport and District, 4.00 for Isle of Thanet, 4.00 for Lanarkshire, 4.00 for the prior lien
   stock of New General Traction, 4.00 for South Staffordshire, 4.50 for Wolverhampton District
   and 5.00 for Worcester Electric Traction, and 6.00 for second mortgage debentures of Perth
   Electric Tramways and of Dundee.
2. The 4 per cent mortgage debentures of London United Tramways (F30) match a separate primary
   record: the Baring Archive holds the prospectus of 18 November 1901 for "825,000 London
   United Tramways 1901 Ltd 4% mortgage debentures and 825,000 5% £10 shares"
   (https://baring.access.preservica.com/uncategorized/SO_f141d0c6-2214-4b03-abc7-5f44fa4bb932/).
3. F28 shows seniority within one company: 3.50 on debentures ranking behind £212,000 of prior
   debentures, against preference capital carrying 6 per cent (non-cumulative).
4. The British Electric Traction figures are holding-company debt; the operating subsidiaries'
   debentures were separate and mostly 4 to 5 per cent.
