# Staff wage evidence, 1710-1946

**Covers**: the measured wage rows used to rebuild `config/staff.tab`, from the general
labourer series of 1710-1911 through the railway, tram and depot grades of 1839-1946. This is
the data appendix to [staff-wages](../staff-wages.md); the reconstruction method and the
resulting series are in [reconstruction](reconstruction.md).

Read with [staff-wages](../staff-wages.md) for the 1901-1910 and 1906-1907 official tables,
the 1912 seamen, the 1956-1968 British Rail and London Transport averages, and the 2019/2026
ASHE figures. This document adds the rows that were missing from that first register.

Rules applied: only what the source states is recorded; no deflation, no annualisation, no
interpolation; where a value is computed it is marked `computed`. A range is recorded as a
range. "Rate" means the recognised time rate; "earnings" means actual pay including overtime.

## 2. Railway driver and fireman combined, 1839

The Stockton and Darlington 0-6-0 *Wilberforce* ran 16,688 miles in 1839; repairs cost
£318 10s 8d (4.5d per mile); the annual wages of the driver and fireman combined were
**£353 12s 8d**. A second post gives **£352 12s 8d** for the same locomotive: a one-pound
variant, not reconciled. Source S12 message 147771, S12 message 169046, S13.

The total is for the crew, not per man. Divided between two men it implies £176 16s 4d each,
4.5 times the general labourer's £39.29 in 1835. That premium is much higher than the 1.5
ratio measured in 1907. The likely explanations are a relief crew, a driver plus two
firemen, or a total that includes other shed labour. The figure is therefore recorded as a
cross-check and is **not** used to set the per-man pre-1850 driver rate.

The 1897 Tilbury total, divided by six locomotives and two men, is £57 16s 8d a man a year,
or 22s 3d a week. That is consistent with the 1897 signalman range and with a driver plus
fireman per locomotive. The 1923 total, divided by nine locomotives and two men, is
£213 8s a man a year, or 82s a week; the post-war wage level is consistent with the Hansard
1922 engine-driver range. These totals are cross-checks, not anchors.

## Assessment

**What is now measured.** The general labourer 1710-1911; the 1907 railway grades; the 1914,
1920, 1930, 1940, 1950 and 1961 railway driver and passenger-porter rates; the same dates for
road passenger transport drivers and conductors; the 1885-1946 signalman record; the 1946
general grades; the 1970-1979 NES means and the 1970-1975 relative pay by grade; and the
2019/2026 ASHE occupations.

**What is still missing.** Fireman, guard and carriage-attendant rates 1926-1968; a measured
driver rate 1962-1968 (the points are differential-derived); road crew before 1890; sea and air
crew before their modern series; and hours.

**The 1839 total** is a combined driver-and-fireman figure that implies a much higher premium
over the labourer than the 1907 ratio; it is not used per man. **The 1946 film** gives spoken
figures rounded to the shilling; it is the best post-war anchor before the 1956 BR table.

## Open questions

- What was the driver and fireman complement of a typical 1840s-1870s locomotive? The 1839
  total cannot be divided without knowing it.
- Do the 1914-1925 Hansard ranges describe rates or earnings? The table says "rates of wages
  of typical classes of time workers"; the engine-driver note says some receive mileage
  allowances in addition, so the range is a rate, not total earnings.
- How many hours did a 1914-1925 engine driver work for the weekly rate? The table does not
  state hours; the 1907 report also omits them.
- Is the 1946 signalman range £4-£6 a basic rate or earnings? The film says "could expect to
  earn", which suggests earnings.
