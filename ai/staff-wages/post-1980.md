# Staff wages, 1980-2050

**Covers**: the measured 1980 New Earnings Survey anchors, the Annual Survey of Hours and
Earnings (ASHE) annual series 1999-2026 for eight occupations, the 1980-1999 bridge, and the
2026-2050 projection that feed `config/staff.tab`.

Read with [staff-wages](../staff-wages.md) for the pre-1980 register and the 1901-1968
tables, and with [reconstruction](reconstruction.md) for the engine conversion, the seven
series and the status of every point.

## 1. The 1980 anchor

The previous file carried a 1980 anchor of unverified provenance: driver £142.77 a week, bus
driver £99.94, labourer £85.66, guard £98.51, fireman £84.80, carriage attendant £92.80, bus
conductor £79.95. Only three of those are independent; the other four are fixed ratios of the
driver or the bus driver (98.51 = 0.69 × 142.77; 84.80 = 0.594 × 142.77; 92.80 = 0.65 ×
142.77; 79.95 = 0.80 × 99.94). `[arithmetic]`

The New Earnings Survey for April 1980 (S19) gives measured mean gross weekly earnings for
full-time men on adult rates, excluding those whose pay was affected by absence:

| Grade in NES 1980 | Weekly | Annual (×52) | Staff type |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Locomotive drivers, motormen | £115.20 | £5,990.40 | staff[0] driver |
| Bus and coach drivers | £117.40 | £6,104.80 | staff[5] bus driver |
| Bus conductors | £104.60 | £5,439.20 | staff[6] conductor |
| General labourers (incl. engineering, shipbuilding) | £94.00 | £4,888.00 | staff[4] labourer |

The NES does not publish a separate railway guard row in 1980; the guard is set at 0.780 of the
driver, the measured 1999 ASHE guard/driver ratio. The fireman (0.594) and carriage attendant
(0.65) remain 1907 ratios.

**The old anchor does not match the survey.** Driver +24 per cent, bus driver −15 per cent,
labourer −9 per cent, conductor −24 per cent. No single basis (rate, earnings, date) reconciles
them, and no source for them was found in this pass. They are replaced by the NES values, which
are measured, internally consistent (all from one table, one date, one population) and
reproducible from the cited page image.

The old driver figure is not, however, absurd: it may be a British Rail main-line driver's
earnings including mileage and overtime, where the NES row also covers London Underground
motormen and tram drivers. The two cannot both be right for the same population, so the
population that matches the modern ASHE grade (train **and tram** drivers) is used.

### Rate or earnings, mean or median

- NES 1980 is **earnings** (overtime, bonus and shift pay included), not a basic rate. ASHE is
  also **gross earnings**. The 1980 anchor and the 1999-2026 points are therefore on the same
  basis, and the pre-1980 rate series joins them at a rate-to-earnings step (section 3).
- NES 1980 is a **mean** for **full-time men**; ASHE is a **median** for **all employees**. The
  mean/median gap for these grades is small: in ASHE 2019 the mean is 1.3 per cent above the
  median for train and tram drivers and 0.9 per cent for bus and coach drivers. The full-time
  male restriction is the larger difference (bus driving in particular has many part-time
  women), and it is not corrected. `[S21; arithmetic]`
- The 1980 NES values are for April 1980, which is the year label used in `staff.tab`.

### Corroboration from 1968

The 1968 driver point is a standard time **rate**, 371.4s a week (£965.77 a year). The 1907
rate/earnings pair puts earnings 10-15 per cent above the rate. Taking £1,060-£1,110 as 1968
earnings and £5,990 as 1980 earnings gives 5.4-5.7 times over twelve years, against a retail
price index rise of 4.04 times (709 to 2,866). That is a real rise of 34-40 per cent for
drivers over 1968-1980, which is large but consistent with the 1968-1980 incomes policy and
productivity deals. The old anchor implies a real rise of 65-73 per cent, which is not.
`[S6, S19; arithmetic; prices.tab general index]`

Notes on the table.

1. **Blanks are ONS suppressions or too-small samples.** Four values used in `staff.tab` are
   interpolated between the adjacent years and are marked `interpolated` in the workbook:
   driver 2001 and 2002; guard 2001 and 2003; labourer 2005. `[arithmetic]`
2. **The classification breaks are visible.** 2011 to 2012: driver +4.8, guard +4.9, bus +1.1,

5. **Air crew in 1999-2001** has no SOC90 equivalent; 631 (railway station staff) is recorded in
   the table only to show that the gap is a classification gap. It is not used.

## 3. The measured years, 1980-1999

The 1980-1999 segment is no longer a constant-rate bridge. S18 was read as page images for
1980, 1982, 1985, 1988, 1990, 1992 and 1995: every value below is the published mean gross
weekly earnings of full-time men on adult rates, **excluding those whose pay was affected by
absence** (Table 86, the second column of "Average gross weekly earnings"). The years 1981,
1983-84, 1986-87, 1989, 1991, 1993-94 and 1996 were not read; the engine interpolates between
the measured years, and 1996 can still be added.

| 1985 | Table 86, D10 and D11 | 191.70 | 171.70 | 134.00 | — | — |

| 1999 | ASHE S20 | 491.48 | 257.52 | 270.52 | — | — |

Notes on the table.

1. **1990 and 1992 publish no rail engine driver row.** They give road transport, sea, plant,
   cranes and "other transport and machinery operatives n.e.c.", and no locomotive line. The
   driver path is therefore interpolated from 1988 to 1995. That is a source gap, not a failure.
2. **The labourer row changes definition after 1988.** To 1988 it is "General labourers
   (including engineering, shipbuilding)"; in 1990 and 1995 the nearest published row is "Other
   building and civil engineering labourers n.e.c.". The 1988 to 1990 rise (+23.8 per cent,
   against +18.0 per cent on the retail price index) includes that definitional change.
   `[arithmetic]` The construction row is used because the post-1999 ASHE proxy is elementary
   construction occupations, so the 1990 and 1995 points connect directly to the 1999 anchor.
3. **1995 also publishes "Rail transport inspectors, supervisors and guards"** (103 in sample,
   £323.50), the same group as ASHE 881 in 1999. It is the only published guard-family figure
   between 1907 and 1999, and it is used as the guard's 1995 point. The group mixes inspectors,
   supervisors and guards, so its level reflects the mix as well as the rate.
4. **The 1982 bus conductor figure (£139.30, 83 in sample) is recorded but not used.** It is
   the last published bus-conductor row, but 83 observations with a wide included/excluded
   spread are thin evidence, and using it would put the conductor at 0.97 of the bus driver in
   1982 and back at 0.891 by 1999 with no measurement in between. The series keeps the
   1980 measured ratio of 0.891, which rests on 132 observations.
5. **The signalman row is a comparator only**, recorded because it is the nearest published
   railway grade to the guard and sits in the same table as the crew rows.

The guard to 1988, the fireman, the attendant and the conductor are ratios of a measured row
(0.7796, 0.594, 0.65 and 0.891); the driver, bus driver and labourer are measured at every date
shown except the driver in 1990 and 1992, where the source has no row.

The measured path is not smooth: the driver gained 15 per cent in real terms over 1985-1988 and
12 per cent over 1988-1995, the bus driver lost 5 per cent over 1990-1995, and the labourer
gained 3 per cent over the same five years. `[S18, prices.tab; arithmetic]` The bridge spread
those movements evenly; the measured points put them where they occurred.

## 4. The projection, 2026-2050

Method: **hold 2026 real pay flat** and rise with the general index in `prices.tab` (16,281 in
2026, 27,224 in 2050, a factor of 1.672). This is the same convention `prices.tab` uses for
fares, mail and goods after 2026, so the ratio of staff cost to revenue stays constant and no
forecast of relative wage structure is embedded in the pakset.

The alternative methods, with their 2050 values:

| Grade | 2026 | Flat real (chosen) | 1999-2026 real trend | +1% a year real |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Driver | 73,874 | 123,527 | 155,535 | 156,846 |
| Guard | 48,180 | 80,563 | 84,857 | 102,293 |
| Bus driver | 33,930 | 56,735 | 62,618 | 72,038 |
| Labourer | 27,675 | 46,277 | 39,659 | 58,759 |

The grade-specific real trend is the measured 1999-2026 real growth compounded for 24 years:
driver +0.86 per cent a year, guard +0.19, bus +0.37, labourer −0.57. Extrapolating it would
carry the driver to 1.26 times the flat-real value and the labourer to 0.86 times, i.e. it
assumes the 1999-2026 relative wage shift continues for another quarter century. That is a
forecast, not a wage-structure fact, and it is rejected for the same reason the pakset does not
extrapolate the 1988-2023 real rise in rail fares. The ±1 per cent band is recorded so the
sensitivity is visible. `[S20, S21; prices.tab; arithmetic]`

**Check.** Every 2030-2050 point is rebuilt from the new 2026 anchor and verified in both
directions: stored × 3/10 / 240 is the annual pound figure, and stored × 3/10 / 624 the implied
weekly rate (for example £123,527 a year, or 47,510s a week, for the driver in 2050). The ×10/3
conversion is applied exactly once.

## 6. Remaining gaps

1. **The NES years not transcribed.** 1981, 1983-84, 1986-87, 1989, 1991, 1993-94 and 1996
   are not listed in `staff.tab`; the engine interpolates between the measured years. 1996 is
   in S18 and could be added; 1990 and 1992 have no published rail driver row at all.
2. **A railway guard anchor for 1980.** The 1980 table has no guard row; the guard is a ratio
   of the driver from 1980 to 1988. The 1995 rail inspectors, supervisors and guards row is
   the first measured guard-family figure and its group mixes three grades.
3. **A carriage-attendant series.** None exists after 1896. The 0.65 ratio implies £48,018 a
   year in 2026, which is far above any catering wage; if the pakset uses the type for buffet
   staff rather than for guards' vans, the ratio should be re-derived from a catering grade.
4. **A modern bus-conductor or conductor-equivalent grade.** None survives in ASHE; the 0.891
   ratio is the 1980 measurement and is held for heritage vehicles.
5. **The 2021 SOC2010 overlap.** The 2021 SOC2010 table would let the 2020/21 break be chained
   rather than spliced.

## Open questions

- Is a mean (1980) to median (1999-2026) splice acceptable, or should the 1999-2026 series use
  ASHE means throughout? The means are published in the same tables and differ by under 2 per
  cent for the grades used here.
- Should the 1980 anchor be a full-time male figure while 1999-2026 is all employees? For bus
  driving the part-time share is large, so the all-employee median is structurally lower.
- Does the pakset want the driver paid average earnings (which is what all these series are) or
  a basic rate? The engine pays a fixed monthly wage with no overtime, so earnings overstate the
  cost of a fixed duty rota by the overtime and mileage content (about 10-15 per cent in 1907,
  unmeasured since).
