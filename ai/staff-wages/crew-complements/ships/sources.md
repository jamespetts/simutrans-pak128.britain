### Named ships whose crew agreements are retrievable

The strongest evidence for the named ships is the crew agreement, not a published history. The
following series should be searched by official number before any figure in this table is
treated as final: BT 99 (10 per cent sample, 1861-1938), BT 100 (celebrated ships, including
Great Eastern), BT 98 (muster rolls to 1856), BT 144 (fishing vessels under 80 tons from 1884)
[S02][S03]. The Maritime History Archive holds about 70 per cent of surviving agreements for
1863-1938 and 1951-1976; the National Maritime Museum holds the years ending in five [S01].

## 4. Gaps and estimates

### Measured rows (eleven)

Eleven rows rest on a published complement for that ship or a directly comparable one:

- `SSGreatWestern` 60 [S12]
- `SSGreatBritain` 130 [S13]
- `SSGreatEastern` 418 [S14]
- `ClipperHull` 30, from Cutty Sark 28-35 [S15]
- `ThamesSailingBargeBulk` 2 [S06][S07][S08]
- `DieselNarrowboatBulk` and the other motor narrowboats 2 [S10]
- `PSWaverley2` 25 [S34][S35][S36]
- `SR-N6-Winchester` 3 [S32]
- `BHC-AP1-88_100` 3 [S33]
- `HSS-1500` 33 [S26]
- `Incat112m` 22 [S30]

`MVBalmoral` at 20 is derived from the statement that about 50 seagoing crew work across
Waverley and Balmoral [S36], not from a Balmoral complement.

### Rule-derived rows

All sailing vessels other than the clipper and the Thames barge; all steam cargo ships; all
historical ferries except the modern ones listed above. These use the Board of Trade manning
returns [S04] or the coastal sailing rule [S05]. They are defensible as class figures but are
not measured for the individual ship.

### Rows with the weakest basis

The following are first approximations: some have no source at all, and the rest are limited
only by a class range or by a measured peer.

1. `EastIndiamanHull` 120 - class range 100-143, but no complement for a specific ship.
2. `HumberKeelBulk` 3 - no measured complement; a sailing keel may have been worked by two.
3. `NorfolkWherryBulk` 2 - no measured complement.
4. `SteamBargeTug` 5 and `WoodenPaddleTug` 6 - no measured tug complement of any date.
5. `SteelHullDumbBargeBulk` 1 and `WoodenHullDumbBargeBulk` 2 - no measured dumb barge crew.
6. `ClydePufferBulk` 4 - puffer crews are usually given as three or four, but no source was read.
7. `PSComet` 6, `PSIndustryPiece` 8, `WoodenPaddleSteamer` 10 - pioneer paddle steamers.
8. `RMSColumba` 30 - a 543 GRT steamer with a 1,500-passenger certificate; the ratio is the
   least secure in the passenger table.
9. `POSpirit` 102 is peer-based (Pride of Kent, MAIB 3/2019); `POPride` 81 is measured for the
   class (Pride of Calais, MAIB 18/2012). See the ferry section below.
10. `HandysizePiece` 35 - a 1958 crew, before the automation that the SIRC figures describe.

**Clan Line wage caveat.** The 63 is the measured lascar crew of *Clan Lamont*. The pakset pays
ratings at the British series, so a Clan Line ship's crew cost is overstated. That is a wage-grade
gap, not a complement error; a separate lascar or Indian rating grade would resolve it. The seven
cargo variants each carry the full 63.

### Data and design gaps

- **Duplicated power in cargo variants.** Twelve files give every cargo fit the same `power`
  (see Scope). If crew is declared on only the first object, buying another cargo fit produces a
  vessel with no crew. The declaration must be repeated on every variant in those files.
- **`engine_type` is unreliable in hold files.** `carferry-holds.dat`, `blackwall-frigate-holds.dat`,
  `clipper-holds.dat`, `brig-holds.dat`, `east-indiaman-holds.dat`, `schooner-holds.dat` and
  `windjammer-holds.dat` carry `engine_type=sail` with zero power. Do not use `engine_type` to
  decide whether an object is a hull.
- **`diesel-light-barge.dat`, `diesel-narrowboat.dat` and `semi-diesel-narrowboat.dat` contain one
  hold with `engine_type=steam` or `Diesel` (capitalised) among otherwise diesel objects.** This
  is a data error, not a second engine.
- **No container ship, tanker or hydrofoil exists in the pakset.** The rules are recorded for
  future objects but no row applies them.
- **`boats/holds/carferry-holds.dat` is used by `island.dat`, `loch.dat` and `superloch.dat`**, not
  by a separate car ferry. The name is misleading but the holds are not orphaned.
- **The pakset `weight` field cannot carry a tonnage rule.** `BlackwallFrigateHull`,
  `ClipperHull` and `EastIndiamanHull` all have `weight=300` although their real tonnages differ by
  a factor of four. Crew must be assigned by class, as in the table.
- **Sea grades now exist in `config/staff.tab`.** Grades 7 (officer), 8 (rating) and 9 (catering)
  were added on 2026-11-23 and every vessel declares a complement. The wage evidence and the
  ten remaining gaps are in [reconstruction](../reconstruction.md) section 2.
- **Crew is charged per vehicle, not per convoy, for water vehicles.** A tug and six barges charge
  the tug crew plus one bargee per barge. The declarations accept that total rather than putting
  the whole complement on the tug; it is the largest approximation in the water fleet.
